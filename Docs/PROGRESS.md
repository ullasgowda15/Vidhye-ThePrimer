## Day 12: what landed

- **`apps/analytics/models.py`** — one table, `SessionReport`. Stored rather than computed on read for three reasons, in order: a report is a claim about one afternoon and recomputing it next month against a changed catalog would quietly rewrite what a parent was told; §17.5 prunes the beats it was derived from; and the highlight costs a model call. `session` is nullable (SET_NULL) while `session_key` is not — the URL key and the idempotency gate both have to survive a retention job clearing the FK. **The weekly report is deliberately not a table** — a stored weekly row is a second thing that can disagree with the sessions it summarises, plus a second idempotency problem, to save a cheap query.
- **`apps/analytics/skills.py`** — the answer to the question Day 10 logged. *Neither* per objective nor per ladder: the number is not shown at all. `reach_for` asks `demands.demand_for` what the hardest ask this objective can make actually is and adds `EVIDENCE_MARGIN`, so `count_to_3` reaches 0.64, `count_to_5` 0.84, `count_to_10` 1.0 — and the status is a *fraction of that reach*. A child who has finished counting to three reads "does this reliably", not "64%". Asking `demands` rather than reading `COUNT_CEILING` here is what keeps this file from having its own copy of the curriculum's arithmetic.
- **`apps/analytics/reports.py`** — almost nothing in a report is generated. Where they went, the key choice, the objective and how it went are rendered from `Beat`, `Choice` and the live `LearningProgress`. `_ask_phrase` reconstructs the ask via `demand_for` from the beat's stamped difficulty, which is only truthful *because* of the Day-10 anchor fix — "counting 3 things" is what was in the picture even when the selector asked for harder. `interest_trend` builds §11.7's "space, then dinosaurs, now ocean" out of the interest snapshots stored on past reports, because the graph itself holds a weight and a date and has no memory of March.
- **`apps/analytics/prompts.py`** — the model writes **one sentence** and nothing else, and `is_grounded` re-checks it against the session's own specifics (the child's name as we hold it, plus something they actually said, chose, answered, or met) before it is stored. An ungrounded sentence falls back to one assembled from columns; a session with nothing to point at never calls a provider at all. `_UNGROUNDING` is what makes the gate a check rather than a formality — without it the word "the" is a specific and every sentence is grounded.
- **`SessionReport.highlight_source`** (`model` / `data` / `quiet`) is on the wire and rendered as a provenance line on the report page. It is the operational window on the gate: all-`data` means it is rejecting everything, no `quiet` in a week means it is rejecting nothing.
- **`apps/analytics/tasks.py`** — the fourth post-session sibling, and the only one with an opinion about order: it snapshots what the mastery and interest updates wrote, so it **gates on `learning_updated_at` and `interests_updated_at`** and retries (3 × 15s) rather than trusting the enqueue order. After that it writes anyway with a `report_stale` warning. The read path (`report_for_session`) generates a missing report on demand, so a broker outage cannot leave a hole in the dashboard.
- **`apps/analytics/transparency.py`** — deliberately thin, and the thinness is the feature. Nothing is summarised, softened or omitted, and `safety_status` is shown so a parent can see the safety layer working rather than take our word for it.
- **Frontend** — `routes/parent/`: `Home` (today's highlight per child), `Reports`, `SessionReport`, `WeeklyReport`, `Insights`, `Transparency`, `Children`, plus `DashboardShell` (calm theme + §12.10 nav) and `dashboard.ts`. `ParentHome.tsx` became `Home.tsx`; its Day-2 children list is now `Children.tsx`. **No number is rendered anywhere on the parent surface**: `mastery` and `reach` cross the wire and `dashboard.test.tsx` scans the rendered DOM for `0.64`, `64` and `%`. `completionRate` renders as "3 played to the end", not "75%". The parent surface *shows* errors — the opposite of the child surface (§13) — and `Section` is where that is written once.

## Day 12 watch-items for the review + manual test

- **The one thing a reviewer should try to break is the grounding gate.** Everything else on this surface degrades safely; a fabricated sentence about a real child does not. `is_grounded` is generous by design (substring matching, phrases *and* their words), and the failure direction chosen was "occasionally passes a vague sentence" over "rejects good writing for using a synonym". A reviewer who disagrees with that trade should say so — it is the judgement call of the day.
- **The scripted provider now answers `SessionHighlight`** (`conftest.py::ScriptedAIProvider.highlight`), reading the digest and quoting something the child actually did. That means CI exercises the *model* path end to end rather than only the monkeypatched one — but it also means the grounding gate is being tested against a provider that is trying to pass it. What a real model does with the prompt is a manual-test question.
- **Two sittings in a row practise different objectives**, by design (§10.3 interleaves domains). It caught two of my own tests out. Any test about how *one* objective moved across a week has to pin the code rather than play twice — `test_reports.py::_snapshot_last_week` is the helper and says why.
- **Milestone status is the least-advanced objective under it, and it can go backwards** — on a child's fourth birthday `count_to_10` joins their curriculum and "Counting and quantity" drops from mastered to practising. That is true and unreadable alone, which is why it is always rendered next to `furthest` ("can count up to five visible things"), which never goes backwards. Worth a second opinion on whether the pair reads right to a parent.
- **`_key_choice` takes the *last* branching beat, not the first.** A five-beat arc opens with a decision that sets direction and closes with one the story built toward; the second is the one a child tells their parent about. Defensible either way.
- **Consent is required on every dashboard read** (`require_consent`), consistent with every other child-data endpoint. Note the consequence: a parent whose consent lapsed cannot read their own child's transparency log. That is the right default today and wants revisiting when the privacy centre lands on Day 13.
- **Not done, deliberately:** no settings or controls, no privacy centre, no billing (Days 13–14); no email delivery of the weekly digest (§12.2's `[C]`); `Memory.deleted_at` is read but nothing writes it yet — parent-facing deletion is the privacy centre's job, and the endpoint already excludes tombstoned rows so that it works the day something does.
- **mypy** reports two new errors in `analytics/views.py`, both the Django reverse-accessor / `_id` false positive that appears ~220 times across the repo already. No new class of error; the step is non-blocking in CI.

## Day 11: what landed
- **`apps/memory/models.py`** — `InterestEdge` (§17.3) and `WorldPlace` (§17.2). Both live in the memory app because §8.3 says the world state *is* memory, and both are derived: `Memory` is what happened, these two are what it added up to. `InterestEdge.child` is `related_name="interest_edges"` — `interests` is already the seed JSON column and Django refuses to start with the clash.
- **`apps/memory/interest.py`** — the graph. Reinforcement is graded (`SPOKE` 0.5 > `CHOSE` 0.35 > `ENCOUNTERED` 0.08), saturating rather than unbounded, and read off the persisted beats/choices rather than the STM log so a Redis outage cannot silently stop it. **Decay is a function, not a job**: a weight means "this much, as of `last_reinforced_at`", every read ages it (30-day half-life), and only reinforced rows are written — so the column keeps saying when the child last *cared* rather than when the job last ran. Faded rows are deleted at the next update. `top_interests` merges the graph with the onboarding seeds (`SEED_WEIGHT` 0.4, no decay), so personalisation works on adventure one and is overtaken by behaviour by adventure three.
- **`apps/memory/tasks.py::update_interests`** — the third post-session sibling, enqueued by `end_adventure` alongside `consolidate` and the mastery update, in its own `try`. Idempotent on `PlaySession.interests_updated_at` (a redelivery would raise the same weights twice, and the graph has no record of which sitting a weight came from).
- **`apps/story/worlds.py`** — four shipped worlds (`whispering_woods`, `star_harbor`, `coral_deep`, `sunlit_canyon`), each with three places. The world is scored from the interest graph; the place is comfort-first (revisit until `NOVELTY_AFTER_VISITS`, then discover). **Every place carries the gentlest ceiling it is suitable at, and `select_place` filters on it** — so §11.6 is structural: a place above the child's ceiling is not somewhere the model was told to avoid, it is somewhere it was never given. Four worlds rather than §15.1's three because the founder test is "dinosaurs vs. ocean" and a dinosaur child landing in a forest is the §2.4 failure arriving quietly.
- **Orchestrator** — `personalize(child)` runs before the plan and bundles the day's four inputs (top interests, known places, recurring cast, fear ceiling) plus the chosen setting; `build_plan` takes it and `story_prompts.plan_request` renders it. The setting is written onto `Adventure.plan` (world, place, mood, `place_id`), the visit is recorded once the row exists, and a character introduced in a beat is written into that place's `inhabitants` in the same transaction.
- **`apps/learning/engine.py`** — Day 10's named landing site, wired: `_interest_tokens` now reads `interest.selector_tokens` instead of the onboarding text box. The term is still a *tie-break* by design (§10.3 puts curriculum at the child's edge); what the graph is load-bearing for is which world the edge is dressed in.
- **Frontend** — `design/worlds.ts` + four palettes and four night variants in `tokens.css`, applied as `data-world` on the document root by `useWorldTheme`. Night is read from the device clock (19:00–06:00) and dims *that world* rather than replacing it. `AdventureStart` gained `world: {key, name, place}` so the palette follows the plan rather than being re-derived on the client.

## Day 11 watch-items for the review + manual test
- **Decay is lazy by design.** Nothing rewrites the weights of topics a sitting did not touch. If a reviewer expects a decay pass over the whole graph, read `interest.py`'s docstring first — the trade was made deliberately and the alternative makes `last_reinforced_at` lie.
- **`build_plan`'s signature deviates from the brief's stub.** The brief names `build_plan(child, top_interests, known_places, recurring_characters, fear_ceiling, objective, difficulty)`; it ships as `build_plan(child, selection, personalization)`. `Selection` already binds objective+difficulty (splitting them is how a beat ends up practising one thing at another thing's difficulty), and the four personalisation inputs are bundled so a caller cannot pass the interests and forget the ceiling. Every named input is present and is asserted in `test_worlds.py`.
- **The fear ceiling is not restated in the plan prompt**, on purpose: rules live in the safety policy layer (`prompting.assembler`), and `story/ai/prompts/story.py`'s docstring forbids restating one. Day 11's contribution is the structural filter above, plus a test that personalisation cannot widen the policy.
- **The world keys cross the stack** (`apps/story/worlds.py` ↔ `frontend/src/design/worlds.ts` ↔ `tokens.css`). Both sides assert the same list; a rename that misses one repaints a child's screen in the default theme, silently.
- **`timezone.now()` on this dev machine resolves to ~1 ms** (2000 consecutive calls → 3 distinct values). Any test that records several `WorldPlace` visits in a loop and then relies on `-last_visited_at` ordering will tie and fall through to `name`. Date the visits explicitly, as `test_a_worn_out_world_still_moves_the_child_around_it` does — real visits are an adventure apart.
- **A new world palette must clear WCAG AA on its own `--bg`.** Tinting a neutral grey toward a world's hue *lightens* it, which is how three of the first four shipped below 4.5:1 on `--text-muted`. `worlds.test.tsx` now computes the ratio from `tokens.css` itself — read that test before adding a fifth world.
- **Not done, deliberately:** no parent-facing interest view (Day 12), companion `evolution_state` untouched (stage 0), and the `context` hint on `POST /adventures` is still validated-but-unread — the world comes from the graph, not from the device.

## Day 10 fix pass: what changed, and what to feel for
- **The anchor.** `demand_for` now clamps `Demand.difficulty` down with the quantity when an objective's `COUNT_CEILING` bites, so the stamped number always describes the ask that was made. A `count_to_5` clean win tops out at an observation of 0.84 instead of climbing to 1.00 across four identical asks; `count_to_3` tops out at 0.64, which still clears `PREREQUISITE_MASTERY` (0.6) and so still opens the next link — on evidence rather than on a float. **Progression up the counting chain is now deliberately slower.** Nothing below a ceiling changed: `count_to_10`, the bottom of the ladder and the two-children-different-difficulty result are byte-identical.
- **Worth a Day-12 thought, not a fix today:** a child who counts to three perfectly asymptotes at ~0.64 mastery, because 3 things *is* a 0.44-difficulty ask on a scale where 10 things is 1.0. Honest, but it is a number a parent will read, so decide then whether the dashboard shows mastery per objective or per ladder.
- **`story.lesson_language`** — a warn-only sibling of the existing consequence/distinctness probes, over `narration` + `prompt_audio`. Fires on the objective code, the rendered difficulty, or `lesson / practice / quiz / test / score / correct answer`. It never blocks or mutates a beat. **Watch this log line during the manual test against a real provider** — it is the only thing that can catch the model saying the quiet part out loud, and the scripted provider will never trigger it.
- **Untouched on purpose:** mastery math, selector scoring, ZPD/spacing/prerequisite logic, `COUNT_LADDER`/`COUNT_CEILING`, the interest graph (Day 11's landing site, still inert — `INTEREST_WEIGHT` scores 0.0 for every realistic parent-entered interest).

## Day 10 watch-items for the review + manual test
- **Two asks per 5-beat story.** Beats 2 and E−1. Fewer would make §10.4's within-session adaptation impossible (nothing to adapt *to*); more would be a worksheet. This is the judgement call most worth a second opinion.
- **Interest fit is deliberately weak** and shipped two bugs on the way in, both now tests: it fired on the stopword "the", and then on the word "frog" appearing as an *example* in the `name_colours` description — which handed a frog-loving child vocabulary instead of counting on their first-ever adventure. Objective descriptions now name the skill and never the scenery. The real version reads the interest graph (Day 11).
- **`interaction.expected` is what makes a beat evidence.** A `choose` beat with a stray `expected` would be phantom evidence, so the `learning` tag — not the interaction type — decides: non-ask beats and safe fallbacks carry `objective_id: "none"` and are skipped.
- **Scaffolding adds company, not a smaller ask.** The difficulty step (`ADAPT_STEP`) already lowers the count; taking another rung off would drop a child from five things to two for one wobble. Tested that a single struggle never crosses more than one rung.
- **The scripted test provider changed shape.** Its opening used to be hard-coded as a counting beat because nothing else produced one; the engine owns that now, so the opening offers cards like a real one. That is why several media/choice tests moved to a different beat.

## Day 9 watch-items for the review + manual test
- **Cross-child leakage is still the automatic P0.** Proved both directions in `test_retrieval.py`, plus `mark_recalled` on a forged bundle touching zero rows. Re-check it anyway with two real children.
- **Semantic recall is unverified.** Running on `local-hash` embeddings, which are *lexical*. The greeting will still name a real memory (rule-includes need no vectors), but *which* one it picks is not semantically chosen until a real embedding key is set. This is the one claim the test suite cannot make.
