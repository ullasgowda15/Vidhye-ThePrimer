## Overall verdict
**NOT-READY** — one P0, and it is the one the review brief was written to catch.

The engineering of this day is done and most of it is done well. A full seven-beat adventure plays start to warm close on the configured model, `end` finalises both rows, the prompt is genuinely flat (13.9k chars at beat 1, 13.7k at beat 6, 14.6k at beat **46**), six real pre-Day-5 beats that used to kill the stream now replay through it cleanly, and the no-fail path ran end to end against a real model for the first time — the child missed a count, was counted along with, and the next beat opened *"Pip and I hold hands and look at the log… We count together: one, two!"* with `emotion=playful_proud` and not one word about a mistake. That is §8.5 working, not §8.5 asserted.

What fails is criterion 1, on the configured model, in the way the founder's own note defines failure: **the consequence beat sometimes narrates an action the child did not take.** Not a generic continuation — a false statement about the child's own choice. Three instances in fourteen real consequence beats (~21%), including two in the adventure Spidey is *currently in*: they tapped "Count the spiders" and were told **"Spidey chose to point at the toy elephant"** — a card that was never even on screen. My own live playthrough reproduced it at beat 4: the child tapped "Count the right frog" and the beat opened "Aarav chose to watch the frogs".

The cause is identifiable and mechanical rather than a vague model-quality complaint — `build_beat_context` hands the model a **fixed plan stage** for `seq` *and* `CONSEQUENCE_RULE` ("Name what they chose"), and when the tap does not point at the stage, the stage wins and the model rewrites the child's action to match it (P0-1). Choices are **not** cosmetic — the controlled A/B produced materially different narration, a different illustration prompt and different follow-on options from one changed line of prompt — so the milder P0 trigger ("choices don't affect the story") is *not* met. But by the standard the brief sets for item 12, M1 cannot be declared.

Second, smaller: the warm close is always warm, always safe and always asks nothing — but **planted a tomorrow hook in 0 of 5 live closes on the configured model** (the two real closes in the database, generated before the model was switched, both have one). `normalise_choices` enforces the *shape* of the close; nothing enforces the hook, and §8.8's reason-to-come-back is the retention mechanic itself.

## What's working well

- **The no-fail path is not a claim any more.** Until today no `count` beat had ever been generated in real play, `assisted` had never once been `True` in the database, and the scaffolding branch had never run against a model. `adff480` is what made criterion 5 *exercisable*, and the beat it produced — hands held, counting together, `playful_proud`, no mention of a miss — is the best writing in this repo. The reasoning in `asks_to_count`'s docstring (a `choose` has no wrong answer, which is the point of it and also why it cannot be the only thing a beat ever asks) is the kind that stops a future refactor from quietly undoing the guarantee.
- **The compaction bound is arithmetic, not a hope.** `SUMMARY_MAX_CHARS` is *derived* from the three constants above it and exported so a test can assert against the number rather than a docstring. Measured on real beats it holds, and at 46 beats it is still 736 of 892. The module docstring is honest about what the fold throws away and why that loss belongs to Day 8's memory rather than to this line.
- **`normalise_choices` enforces at the write boundary, not at the read.** Cards on the close, cards on a `count`, a fourth card, two cards with one key — all settled once, where the model's output becomes our data, so the client, the TTS text, the transcript a parent reads and Day 8's memory all trust one clean record. The comment explaining *why* cards on a `count` beat are fatal (the client picks the number pad by the absence of options, so a stray card removes the only control that can answer) is the reason this is a fix and not a tidy-up.
- **The legacy coercion is safe rather than merely non-crashing.** The empty `icon_prompt` is load-bearing: `media_stages` skips an option without one, so a two-day-old beat replays with picture-less cards instead of spending three provider calls illustrating labels nobody wrote to be drawn. I confirmed that on all six real rows.
- **Prefetch refuses to guess.** `ADVANCE_BRANCHING` is a decision to spend nothing, and the docstring names both reasons — a guessed branch is a beat the child never sees, and a `count` can still come back `assisted`, which the next beat must react to. A cheaper implementation would have looked identical in every test and quietly paid for half its beats.
- **`next_position` means "lowest unanswered", not "newest row".** That one choice is what makes prefetch safe, and the test that pins it (`test_a_prefetched_beat_does_not_skip_the_one_the_child_is_on`) describes the bug it prevents — the child watching a beat they never got to play.
- **The whole choice surface has no failure state to find.** No `expected` check on the server, no disabled card, no red, no correct answer revealed afterwards, no score on the close. I went looking with grep and with a scan of every beat ever generated, and there is nothing there.

## Punch-list (hand this to the next Build session)

### P0 — blockers

- [ ] **The consequence beat sometimes tells the child they did something they did not do.** Three of fourteen real consequence beats on the configured model (~21%), two of them in Spidey's **currently in-progress** adventure: they tapped "Count the spiders" and were told *"Spidey chose to point at the toy elephant"* — a card that was never offered. A pre-reader cannot contest that, and §8.5's entire promise is that the world reacts to what *they* did. **Root cause, and it is mechanical rather than a vague model complaint:** `build_beat_context` sends the model both a **fixed plan stage** for this `seq` (`orchestrator.py:416-417`) and `CONSEQUENCE_RULE`'s instruction to *name what they chose*. When the tap does not point at the stage, the stage wins and the model reconciles the conflict by rewriting the child's action — sometimes literally, with the words "you chose to". In all three cases the narrated action was the assigned stage's action; in the eleven correct cases the tap and the stage happened to agree. Note that divergence itself is **fine** — the controlled A/B above is materially different in narration, illustration and follow-on options — so this is not "choices are cosmetic". It is worse and narrower: choices are real but sometimes misreported. **Fix directions, cheapest first:** (a) tell the model the stage is a *destination to reach from where the child has taken us*, not a script for this beat, and that if the two disagree the child's action is the fact and the stage bends; (b) forbid the construction "you chose to X" unless X is the label in `Chose:` — the label is right there in the prompt; (c) belt-and-braces, a cheap post-write check that flags a beat naming an **un-chosen** option's label (the labels are on the row) — not to block the beat, but to log `story.consequence_misattributed` so the rate is visible instead of discovered by a parent. Whatever the fix, item 12 needs re-running before M1 is declared. **Criterion 1 and criterion 8's "M1 reached" both hang on this.**

### P1 — should fix

- [ ] **The warm close plants no tomorrow hook on the configured model — 0 of 5 live.** §8.8 and criterion 6 both say *always*, and `FINAL_BEAT_RULE` asks for it in as many words ("plant one small hook for tomorrow — something the companion has found or noticed"). The model complies with the *shape* every time (`type: none`, no cards) because `normalise_choices` makes it, and ignores the hook every time because nothing does. Live closes: *"…walk home hand in hand, talking about the smooth stones they found"*, *"…the frogs' song fades into a sleepy hum"*, *"…now the whole frog chorus is singing just for us, Aarav!"*, *"…ready to sing."* — all warm, all safe, none giving a four-year-old a reason to ask for tomorrow. The two closes in the database from before the model switch both have one, so this is a model-behaviour regression the prompt no longer wins. Worth noting the asymmetry: the close is the one beat whose *shape* is enforced server-side and whose *substance* is not. Cheapest fix is to make the hook a separate, named sentence the prompt demands last ("End with one sentence beginning with what you will look for tomorrow"); a validator that regenerates a hookless close is the expensive version and probably not worth a second call.

- [ ] **Three cards that are the same action are three cards that cannot diverge.** Live beat 3 offered "Count the left frog" / "Count the middle frog" / "Count the right frog", and beat 4 duly ignored which one was tapped (it is one of the P0-1 instances). §14.5's two-or-three options are meant to be *different things that happen next* — `CHOICE_RULE` says so — but nothing checks it, and cosmetic variants are the condition under which P0-1 is guaranteed rather than likely. This is cheap to influence from the prompt side (each option must lead somewhere the *other* options do not) and cheap to notice in the log (three labels sharing a leading verb). Fixing it removes a chunk of P0-1's surface even before P0-1 itself is addressed.

### P2 — improvements (non-blocking, log for later)

- [ ] **The legacy-shape tests are synthetic, which is the thing the brief asked not to happen.** All three tests in `TestABeatStoredInAnOlderShapeStillPlays` construct unsaved `Beat(interaction={...})` objects; none loads a persisted row and none goes through the stream. The coercion is correct — I verified it against all six real rows on the real SSE path — but the *regression* the tests are meant to prevent lived specifically in "a stored row reaches `streaming.choice_payload` mid-response", and that path is not covered. A fixture that saves a beat with bare-string `choices` and drives `stream_beat` over it would cost about ten lines and would actually fail if `Beat.options` regressed.
- [ ] **The `beat` frame still ships the raw, un-normalised `interaction` blob.** `beat_payload` sends `interaction: beat.interaction` verbatim (`streaming.py:156`), so a legacy beat puts `"choices":["go outside","jump up and down"]` on the wire while the top-level `choices` array carries the coerced objects — and `BeatEvent.interaction.choices` is typed in `sse.ts:57` as `{key,label,icon_prompt}[]`. Nothing reads it today (`StoryPage` uses `beat.choices`, `beat.interaction.type` and `.expected` only), so nothing is broken; but the frame contract is currently lying about its own type, and the first client code that trusts the declared type re-opens exactly the bug `21a9a4d` closed. Either normalise on the way out or drop `choices` from the interaction blob, since the top-level field supersedes it.
- [ ] **What the child answered never reaches the running summary.** `Choice.value` is carried into the *next* beat's prompt via `turn_from` → the assembler's `Answered: 2` line, and then lost: `summary._entry` records only the chosen key's label, so the count beat folds into continuity as `Aarav chose 'count'.` — the internal key, and no number. The one piece of evidence the numeracy objective exists to generate survives exactly one beat. Day 10 will want it, and it is two lines in `_entry`.
- [ ] **`consequence_summary` is computed, stored, passed into the prompt builder and then silently dropped.** `turn_from` puts `"consequence": choice.consequence_summary` in the turn dict (`orchestrator.py:396`), and `assembler._turn_block` has no branch that renders it. Nothing is lost in practice — the same facts arrive as `Chose:` and the `next_hint` line — but a field that is threaded three layers deep and then ignored will be read as live by whoever touches this next. Either render it or stop passing it.
- [ ] **The comment in `config/urls.py` promises a CDN that does not exist.** *"In production the CDN serves `STATIC_ROOT`"* — there is no whitenoise, no storage backend for static, and `STATIC_URL`/`STATIC_ROOT` are the bare defaults. True that DEBUG-off means Django serves nothing; not yet true that anything else does. Worth a word in `DEPLOY.md` before the first deploy, so the admin is not discovered unstyled in staging.
- [ ] **Nothing records which model wrote a beat.** The single most useful column for this review would have been `Beat.model`, and there is none — so "did nemotron or gemma produce this misattribution" had to be inferred from option counts and `next_hint` phrasing. Given that item 12's whole failure class is model-dependent and the router already supports a fallback chain, a provider/model stamp on the row is cheap now and un-backfillable later.
