What “{{ days }} days” means, and what the clock changes

The window is rolling, not a fixed calendar period. “{{ days }} days” means the {{ days }} × 24 hours ending right now — {{ window.since|date:"j M Y, H:i" }} to {{ window.until|date:"j M Y, H:i" }} UTC — and it is recomputed from scratch every time the page loads. Reload in an hour and both ends have moved by an hour. It is not a snapshot taken once, and it is not last calendar week, so a number here is “the last {{ days }} days as of this moment” and that is how it should be quoted.

Two consequences worth knowing. Both ends are partial days — the window opens part-way through its first day and closes part-way through today, so the first and last bars in “Day by day” cover only some hours and are always short. Neither is a downturn; both are marked part in the table. And a metric can change without anyone doing anything: events aging out of the far end of the window move it just as new events do.

The clock is a display setting only. It decides which local hour and which calendar day an event is filed under, so it changes the hour charts, the heatmap, the day-by-day table and the timestamps in the roster. It moves no rate, funnel, cohort or retention number — those are computed on the server's own UTC days so that a figure cannot change because somebody picked a different dropdown.

Why this grid and not just the hour chart

A product used at 19:00 on weeknights and 10:00 at weekends has an identical hour-of-day profile to one used at both times every day. Why position is the check that decides what every other number here means

If four-year-olds are tapping the first card without reading it, then the completion rate, the learning signal and every mastery estimate built on top of them are measuring a reflex. Nothing else on this dashboard would show that — a reflexive tap and a considered choice produce identical rows everywhere else. That is why this panel is a design check rather than a metric: there is no target, and the only question is whether the bars sit near their chance marks.

Chance is computed, not assumed. Beats offer two or three options and the mix moves week to week, so the fair share for the first card is the average of 1 ÷ (options offered) across the choices that actually offered one — not a flat third. Measured against a flat third, a week heavy in two-option beats would read as a first-card bias that is pure arithmetic. × is taken ÷ chance: ×1.00 is exactly chance.

A position that reads all zeros is a finding, not a gap. Rows run to the highest position ever offered, so a third option nobody ever picks gets a row of zeros — and an option children never take is worth going and reading.

Choices carrying no position are excluded rather than counted as the first card: those are events written before option_index existed, plus the genuine cases (an unrecognised key, or a beat offering only “carry on”). Counting them as position 0 would draw a first-card habit out of history.

Why the parents’ settings matter more than they look

session_cap_min is the parent’s own answer to “how long should my child be on this”, and intensity_ceiling is their answer to “how brave is my child”. Both are given freely at setup and neither is a guess — the distribution of them says more about the market than any engagement chart here. Glossary

Active
A child who started an adventure. Starting counts; finishing is a separate metric.
Sitting / session
One continuous period of play. It can span more than one adventure, and it is the unit cost is measured per.
Beat
One turn of the story: some narration, and something for the child to do.
Cohort
The children whose first finished story fell in the same week. Never mixed, which is what lets one be compared with another.
DWA — Delighted Weekly Active
A child who completed three or more adventures in a week. The North Star. A count of children, never a measure of time spent.
Reach curve
Of the adventures that got a first beat, how many got an nth. The chart that names the beat people leave at.
Quiet
Hours since an account last did anything. The churn column.
Generation / span
One call to a model that cost money — a beat, an illustration, a line of speech, an embedding. One traced row each.
Unattributed spend
A generation that could not be tied to a sitting. Real money, excluded from cost-per-session, reported separately so the headline is not flattered.
Unpriced generation
A model with no entry in the price table. Counted at zero and flagged, so an unknown price can never be mistaken for a free one.
Matured
A child or a week old enough to be judged. A child whose first story was this morning cannot yet have returned tomorrow, so they are left out of the denominator rather than counted as a miss.
Fallback beat
A beat served from the pre-vetted pool because a generation did not pass the safety layer. The child sees a good story either way; a rising share is the signal.

Metric definitions, and the judgement call inside each one, are written down in apps/analytics/metrics.py and apps/analytics/insights.py; prices are list prices from apps/analytics/pricing.py, not quotes. The same numbers print in a terminal with python manage.py metrics --days {{ days }} --tz {{ zone }}.

Product analytics and the parent-facing dashboard are separate by design. Nothing on this page is per-child; the roster names account holders, which is the customer record, and says so where it sits. This sign-in is independent of the Django admin and grants no access to it.
